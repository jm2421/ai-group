02 · Deep dive

John's Live Every Day

A patient-built, AI-supported companion for navigating rare and watch-and-wait diseases.

The problem

Watch-and-wait cancers mean a 30-minute appointment every 180 days and nothing in between. No real symptom tracking, no communication channel, and patterns get lost. The same gap exists for CLL, indolent lymphoma, active-surveillance prostate cancer, and early-stage thyroid cancer. That's millions of patients.

What it does

  • Tracks symptoms, meds, labs, and wearables. Upload ten years of MyChart history as a PDF and it parses straight into the database.
  • Builds patterns over time, then prepares you: a doctor brief with the questions to ask at your next appointment.
  • Endgame: a real-time dashboard so a doctor's nurse team can watch a whole patient panel and call the ones trending the wrong way.

How he built it

  • One Claude Project running since April 19. Brainstorm to brand brief to logo system to working simulation, all in conversation.
  • Moved to Cowork for file management and direct GitHub pushes. Chat couldn't do that part.
  • Two-person dev team now. Android app live in testing. iOS development started this week.

03 · Discussion

What we debated

The threads worth pulling on again.

Taste vs. iteration

Crowdsourced design converges on vanilla. John's PhD-mathematician ad experiment concluded the perfect ad was "slightly more purple." And Claude thinks every idea you have is brilliant. The pattern that works: a human sets the taste, locks the system (palette, fonts, rules), and then lets AI produce endlessly against it.

Humans as bridges

App Store review, TestFlight gates, D-U-N-S numbers, 501c3 filings. Claude walks you all the way to the bridge, but a person still has to cross it. With vibe-coded slop flooding the app stores, the gates are getting tighter, not looser.

Personal software Al's tease for next time

The pendulum: on-prem, then SaaS, then maybe your own app. Once someone can replicate a fully customized Salesforce in a weekend, including the harnesses to move the data over, what's actually sticky? Full argument at Session 4.
