An AI guide, and a single source of truth

Using AI as a guide on my health, and one website as the record of what I actually do: day to day, week to week, month to month, year to year.

Presenter
Jeb Morrison
Format
Slides, then the live site
Caveat
The health choices are mine. The method travels.
The problem

The plan lived in six places, and the AI could see none of them

  • A spreadsheet of supplements, a prescriber's plan, a wearable app, a glucose-monitor app, lab PDFs, and memory. No two agreed on what I was actually doing on a given Thursday.
  • Every AI conversation started from zero. Paste the sheet, re-explain the constraints, hope the model held it all. Advice was only as good as what I remembered to include.
  • The worse failure: a good proposal in a chat quietly became "the plan" without ever being adopted. Aspiration and record blurred.
  • The fix: one private website, built from data files, that both I and the AI read. The data records what I do. Proposals stay labelled proposals until I confirm them.
Single source of truth

One site, built from data, that records what I actually do

Supplements
Forty-seven rows, every one graded
Dose, timing, mechanism, evidence grade, cost per day, and the reasoning for every change kept in a log.
Injectables and cycles
Ongoing versus cycling
What runs every week, what is a cycle with a state beside it, and the rule: one new pharmacological variable per four-week block.
Training, cardio, mobility
Run sheets, not intentions
Three lifts, three cardio shapes, mobility before every lift, tiered so the cut is pre-decided. A travel page assembles a session from whatever gym is there.
Sleep, nutrition, recovery
Targets with reversal conditions
Bedtime before compounds; protein first; heat, cold and light with the days they belong to.
Cost
From twelve months of receipts
Not from the sheet's estimates. The two are reconciled on the page, gap shown, cause not guessed.
Labs
A watch list with dates
Each marker with the reason it is on the list and the draw it is due at.
Integrity
Every number traces
A hand-typed total fails the build. Every prescription-shaped figure either comes from the data or carries a written reason.
Two builds
Private, and a public subset
The public site is built from a copy with private rows removed. A scanner refuses to publish if a private term survives.

Monitoring

Four clocks: the day, the week, the month, the year

Day
The Today tab
What is on for today, each item clickable into its detail. The wearable's recovery score decides the cut before the session starts: amber drops the last block, red means walk.
Week
The calendar
Three lifts, cardio, mobility, injection days and the wind-down on one grid. Seven hours of full attention; the rest happens alongside a call or a podcast. Two travel sessions keep a week whole.
Month
Four-week blocks
One new pharmacological variable per block. Deload every fourth week, or early on triggers. Glucose-monitor and wearable data overlaid to see what moved what.
Year
The September pass
Would I start this today, and has the evidence moved? A fitness test with reversal thresholds written in advance. Labs on a schedule. A removed-items log so reasoning outlives memory.
Each clock reads the same data files. Nothing is copied between them, so the day view cannot disagree with the year view.

The AI as guide

Reviewer, interpreter, auditor, gatekeeper. Never author.

Reviewer
The whole system at once
Three prompted personas of the same model, none a clinician: a data-driven scientist, an efficiency expert holding weekly time constant, a recovery specialist. Fourteen proposals came out. I accepted, rejected or modified each one.
Interpreter
Reads the data streams
Glucose-monitor and wearable exports side by side: how lifting, cardio, sleep and disturbed nights showed up in glucose, heart rate and variability. Hypotheses to check, not thresholds.
Auditor and gatekeeper
No floating numbers, no leaks
Every figure traces to the data or carries a reason. The public build is scanned for private terms before anything leaves.
  • Prep for the humans: the review produces an agenda for my clinician, not a prescription, and a four-page context summary so a second, specialist AI starts informed rather than from zero.
  • What it never does: set a dose, change a rule without sign-off, or see logins and account secrets. Anything symptomatic goes to the clinician, not the rules page.
  • What it got wrong: one reviewer's proposed schedule got written into the data as what I do. Walked back, and the rule was written down.
Evidence

Two kinds of row: proven, and plausible, unproven

  • Every supplement row carries a grade, A to D, with the AI checking the citations and proposing downgrades; I applied the ones I agreed with. Rows with human evidence are kept or cut on it. About forty percent of supplement spend sits in the two thinnest tiers, stated head-on.
  • The rest are a named category: safe, mechanistically plausible, no valid proof of efficacy. Eleven supplement rows and most of the synthetic peptides. Each carries, on the page, the basis it is kept on and what, if anything, a lab draw might show.
  • Why no trial of one: the outcomes these aim at (aging, mitochondrial quality, long-run cognition) would need trials of thousands of people over years. Twelve weeks of one person cannot confirm or refute any of them, and keeping or dropping one on that basis would be theatre.
  • Why write the basis down anyway: when a validated aging clock, a real trial or a cheap functional assay arrives, the list to re-examine is already there with the reason each row was included.
A correction. An earlier version of this deck framed these rows as twelve-week experiments with markers. That took the "experimental group" metaphor literally; the metaphor is about accepting that we will not know, not about running a trial of one.

What the data streams showed

Ask whether two instruments measure the same thing

  • The inversion that was not one: the AI flagged a wearable's sleeping heart rate sitting above a second device's awake resting rate. The review had compared unlike quantities: a weighted nightly average against a daytime floor. An average sits above a floor for anyone. The error is on the page next to the original claim.
  • The glucose monitor changed one thing on day two. Fasted morning readings under sixty, with a glucose-lowering supplement taken at night riding through the fast with nothing to act on. It moved to late morning with food. A change the data made, recorded as such.
  • Journal tags over-report. The wearable's journal counted alcohol on forty-plus nights in a year; the true number is about fifteen. Defaults, not entries. That comparison was voided rather than reported.
  • What remains is a hypothesis: a slow drift in one series over six months, labelled as one. Next: four weeks of tagged nights.
Guardrails

Five rules that keep the guide a guide

  1. The data records what I do. Proposals stay labelled proposals until I confirm them.
  2. Write the reversal condition before making the change, and put it on the page.
  3. Grade every claim. Separate proven from plausible, and write down the basis for the plausible.
  4. Every number traces to the data or carries a written reason. A hand-typed total fails the build.
  5. The AI proposes; I decide; my clinician sees the same list. It sets no dose and sees no secrets.
Next

Less logging, more reading

  • Automatic capture where a connection exists: the wearable by its own interface, glucose through a bridge, labs by PDF. Food as exceptions only. The aim is a record that does not depend on me remembering to log.
  • A comprehensive outside panel arrives soon; its results get reconciled against the lab watch list rather than read in isolation.
  • Four weeks of tagged nights, then the glucose and wearable overlay again with the tags trusted.
  • The site as the place to talk to the AI, not a chat that starts from zero: recording, and asking questions of the record. Only where that is worth more than coming back to the conversation.
This week

Four things to try

  1. Pick one thing you manage from memory and several apps. Put the record in one place the AI can read, and separate what you do from what you intend.
  2. Run three opposed reviewers against that record: the one who wants more, the one who holds your time constant, the one who worries about what breaks.
  3. Grade your own spend or roadmap. Split proven from plausible, and write the basis for each plausible line in one sentence.
  4. Write the reversal condition for one decision you made this quarter, before the next data point arrives.
