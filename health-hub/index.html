Evidence, and the experimental group

One person's health system, not a protocol. How the choices got made, what the AI was allowed to check, and where the human stays in charge.

Presenter

Format

Caveat

The pattern

Five rules. The rest of the deck is the evidence.

  1. Fix the weekly time budget. Then the only decision is what a rule cuts.
  2. Treat the inputs as separate budgets. Cardio does not buy back sleep.
  3. Grade every claim, and put a number on the share that is a guess.
  4. Write the reversal condition before making the change.
  5. Use AI as a hostile reviewer of the whole system. It proposes; I decide.

Rule 1 · Strength

Pre-decide the cut, so nothing is negotiated at 6am

  • Every session reads in priority order. The last block is the first thing to cut. The arithmetic is on the page.
  • The wearable's recovery score drives the cut. Amber: drop the last block. Red: walk only.
  • Deload every fourth week, or early on triggers: a run of red days, a sustained drop in heart-rate variability against a monthly baseline, or a missed rep on the main lift of the day.
  • Control before power. New movement patterns run two weeks before the explosive work is added. If something flares, you have a candidate cause, not proof.

Rule 4 · Cardio

Write the reversal before the change

  • The change: in September, Friday stopped being a second hard interval session and became an easy ride (zone 2: a pace you can hold a conversation at).
  • Why: the plan had no easy day, and the AI recovery review flagged that as the bigger gap than anything in the hard sessions. Four hard sessions a week became three.
  • The reversal is pre-written on the page: if the next fitness test shows power down past a threshold set in advance, Friday goes back to intervals.
  • Why protect this budget at all: higher aerobic fitness is associated with living longer in population data. A reason to keep the hours. Not a guarantee about one Friday ride.
Rules that can fail

Tag once, let the rules read the tags, test that they can fail

  • Every exercise in the catalogue carries warning tags: which joint it loads, which range it goes through.
  • A flare rule reads the tags. Tight: substitute the one lift, keep the session. Sore: the unloaded half only. Symptomatic: everything with that tag comes out, across lift days and mobility work. The skip list is computed when the site is built, not maintained by hand. These are my rules, reviewed with my clinician, not physio advice.
  • The test that mattered: every tag could be inverted without changing a page. The tests only checked the tags existed. Now they assert consequences: flip one tag, and a named exercise must leave a named page, or the build fails.

Rule 3 · Evidence versus the experimental group

Put a number on the guesses

  • Every supplement row carries a grade from my own inventory, A to D. The AI's job was to check the citations behind the grades and propose downgrades where a grade outran its evidence. I applied the ones I agreed with.
  • Stated head-on: about a third of supplement spend sits in the two thinnest tiers. That is the size of the bet on being in the experimental group.
  • The slot policy: a small number of twelve-week experimental slots, each with a marker written down in advance (a measured number, a test result, or a logged felt effect) that would show it worked. One variable per block.
  • A removed-items log keeps the reasoning, so it survives the person who remembers it. Not yet exercised.
  • What a study of one person can do: produce hypotheses and stop conditions, not evidence. Joining the experimental group means accepting that, and my clinician sees the same list.
Grades: A strong human evidence · B human signal plus mechanism · C works in theory or in a dish, thin human data · D speculative.

Sleep

When two instruments disagree, ask whether they measure the same thing

  • The AI review flagged an inversion: one wearable's sleeping heart rate sat above a second device's awake resting rate.
  • The catch: the review had compared unlike quantities. One number is a weighted nightly average, the other a daytime floor. An average sits above a floor for anyone. The error is on the page next to the original claim.
  • What remains is smaller and untested: a slow drift in one series over six months. That is a hypothesis, labelled as one.
  • Next: four weeks of tagged nights (sauna, alcohol, late activity). They will produce hypotheses to check, not thresholds.
Rule 5 · What the AI was for, and was not

Reviewer, auditor, gatekeeper. Never author.

Reviewer
The whole system at once
Three prompted personas of the same model, none a clinician: an experimental-but-data-driven scientist, an efficiency expert holding weekly time constant, a recovery specialist. Fourteen proposals came out. I accepted, rejected or modified each one.
Auditor
No floating numbers
Every figure on every page must trace to the data or carry a written reason. A hand-typed total fails the build. Data integrity, not decision quality.
Gatekeeper
The privacy line
The public site is built from a copy with the private rows already removed. A scanner sits between the build and anything public and refuses if it objects.
  • What it got wrong: one reviewer's proposed schedule got written into the data as what I do. Walked back. The rule now: the data records what I do; proposals stay labelled proposals until I confirm them.
  • Who decides: I do. The review produces an agenda for my clinician, not a prescription. Anything symptomatic goes to the clinician, not the rules page. The AI sets no dose and changes no rule without sign-off.
  • What the model sees: the plan data and the site's pages. What it never sees: logins and account secrets.
This week

Three things to try

  1. Run three opposed reviewers against one live document of yours. Template briefs: the experimenter who wants more, the efficiency expert who holds your time constant, the specialist who worries about what breaks.
  2. Write the reversal condition for one decision you made this quarter, before the next data point arrives.
  3. Grade your own spend, or your roadmap, A to D. Set the share of C and D you would accept. Then compare.
